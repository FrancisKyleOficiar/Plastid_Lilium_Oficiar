# Part E. Questions to Answer
1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?
Scientific name: _Lilium lancifolium_
Family: _Liliaceae_
Plastid genome accession number: OR400160.1

The selected genome is a complete plastid genome of Lilium lancifolium and has a total length of 152,575 bp.

2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?
Total length: 152,575 bp

The Galaxy FASTA Statistics analysis showed one sequence record with a total length of 152,575 bp. The sequence also contained 0 ambiguous bases (N) and 0 gaps, supporting its use as a continuous complete plastid sequence.

3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.
LSC (Large Single-Copy region): The largest portion of the plastome, located between IRb and IRa.
SSC (Small Single-Copy region): The smaller single-copy region located between the two inverted repeats.
IRa (Inverted Repeat A): One duplicated region located between the SSC and LSC.
IRb (Inverted Repeat B): The second duplicated region located on the opposite side of the plastome.

Published _L. lancifolium_ plastomes report an LSC of approximately 82,005–82,084 bp, an SSC of approximately 17,513–17,585 bp, and IRs of approximately 26,492 bp each. These values vary slightly among accessions.

4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Examples of genes located in the LSC region include:

psbA — encodes the D1 reaction-center protein of Photosystem II.
rbcL — encodes the large subunit of Rubisco and functions in carbon fixation.
atpA — encodes a subunit of ATP synthase involved in ATP production.

Other genes in the LSC include various psa, psb, pet, rpo, rpl, and rps genes. The majority of plastid genes occur within the LSC.

5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.

One example is ndhF, which is located in the SSC region and encodes a component of the chloroplast NADH dehydrogenase-like complex.

Other genes associated with the SSC include several ndh genes, such as ndhA, ndhD, ndhG, ndhH, ndhI, and ndhJ, depending on the particular plastome annotation.

6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

One example is rpl2.

Yes. Genes located within the inverted-repeat regions occur in two copies, with one copy in IRa and another copy in IRb. Other duplicated genes include rpl23, rps7, ndhB, ycf2, the four major rRNA genes, and several tRNA genes.

The duplication occurs because IRa and IRb are two copies of the same genomic region arranged as inverted repeats. Published Lilium plastomes specifically report that all four rRNA genes are duplicated within the IR regions.

7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.
Gene name: psaA
Functional group: Photosystem I
General function: psaA encodes one of the core proteins of Photosystem I. It participates in the photosynthetic light reactions by forming part of the Photosystem I reaction center and supporting light-driven electron transfer.

Another visible photosynthesis-related gene is psbA, which encodes the D1 protein of Photosystem II.

8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The orientation of the genes indicates that plastid genes are encoded on both DNA strands and therefore can be transcribed in different directions around the circular genome. In an OGDRAW representation, genes displayed on opposite sides of the circular map indicate opposite transcriptional orientations.

This organization allows genes located on different DNA strands to be represented according to their respective transcriptional directions. Published Lilium genome maps similarly show genes on both sides of the circular genome according to their transcriptional orientation.

9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.

The GC content is not completely uniform across the plastid genome. The map's inner GC-content graph shows local variation across the LSC, SSC, and IR regions.

The overall GC content measured from the Galaxy FASTA Statistics analysis is 37.03%. Published Lilium plastomes also show an overall GC content of approximately 37%. The IR regions tend to have higher GC content than the single-copy regions.

The difference should be described as a sequence-composition pattern rather than interpreted as evidence of a particular biological function without additional analysis.

10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical plastid genome map makes the overall organization of the genome easier to understand. It allows the positions of the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions to be seen immediately, while also showing the locations of protein-coding genes, tRNA genes, rRNA genes, and other conserved genes.

The map also makes it easier to recognize gene orientation, IR-associated gene duplication, gene distribution, and variation in GC content. In contrast, a raw DNA sequence or a long annotation list provides the sequence information but makes the spatial organization of the genome much harder to visualize.
