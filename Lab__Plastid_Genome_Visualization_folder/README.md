# Main structural features observed

The Lilium lancifolium plastid genome exhibits the typical angiosperm plastid genome quadripartite architecture, consisting of a large single-copy (LSC) region, a small single-copy (SSC) region, and two inverted repeat regions designated IRa and IRb. The genome is approximately 152,575 bp in length and contains the expected plastid genes, including those encoding photosystem I and II subunits, ATP synthase components, ribosomal proteins, ribosomal RNAs, and transfer RNAs. Gene distribution is uneven across the genome, with the LSC containing the greatest number of unique genes, while the IR regions contain duplicated genes and contribute to the conserved structure of the plastome. The overall organization is highly conserved and follows the typical structure reported for Lilium plastid genomes, with no major structural rearrangements evident from the genome map.
